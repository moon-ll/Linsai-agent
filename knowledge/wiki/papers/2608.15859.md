# Anomalous radiation pressure in strong-field ionization driven by quantum light

**arXiv**: [2608.15859](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15859) | **方向**: 强场非平衡态动力学

## 深度解析

### 研究问题（Research Question）

Theory paper establishing that bright squeezed vacuum (BSV) driving changes a foundational strong-field scaling law: the mean longitudinal photoelectron momentum (nondipole radiation-pressure signature) scales as I^(2/3) instead of linear I. The mechanism is a field-amplitude saddle-point selection: nonlinear tunneling picks two equal-magnitude opposite-sign field amplitudes from the broad quantum fluctuations; tracing over photons erases their relative phase, leaving the common magnitude to set the momentum shift. Photon-number resolution restores coherence access via half-cycle double-slit interference.

### 研究背景（Background）

Core relevance to the bsv program - this is the quantitative theory of how BSV driving modifies a strong-field observable beyond intensity statistics. Three hooks: (1) The I^(2/3) law gives the bsv program a falsifiable, spectrometer-ready prediction that distinguishes quantum-light driving from intensity-equivalent classical driving - exactly the kind of experimental anchor the program needs (alongside the attosecond metrology of bright quantum light). (2) The equal-magnitude-opposite-sign saddle selection is a general structural feature of tunneling nonlinearity meeting broad amplitude distributions - it should reappear in BSV-driven solid HHG, where the two branches sample the band structure at +/- k, making the harmonic yield a coherence-weighted probe of valley antisymmetry (connects to project D: BSV-driven sigma_solid would test whether K/K-prime responses are truly symmetric). (3) The photon-number-resolved double slit is the strong-field sibling of the PINEM which-path coherence measurement (2610.08325) - both convert photon-statistics resolution into interference visibility; the pair brackets the free-electron and bound-electron versions of quantum-light coherence witnessing.

### 研究方法（Methods）

Joint light-front saddle-point treatment of quantum-light strong-field ionization (photon statistics + nondipole momentum in one framework); photon-number resolution as coherence-restoring measurement

### 主要发现与核心结论（Main Results）

- Anomalous scaling law: <p_z> proportional to I^(2/3) under BSV driving vs linear under coherent driving - a quantum-light fingerprint in a nondipole observable, measurable with existing photoelectron spectrometers
- Joint light-front saddle-point analysis: treating coherent-state amplitudes beta/beta-bar as independent variables, stationarity yields beta_s ~ +/- i*sqrt(n) - the tunneling nonlinearity selects two dominant amplitudes of equal magnitude and opposite sign from the BSV's broad amplitude distribution
- Coherence erasure mechanism: tracing over the final photon state removes the relative-phase information between the two branches; only the common magnitude survives, and the BSV amplitude statistics convert the linear-in-amplitude response into the 2/3-power-in-intensity law
- Photon-number-resolved regime: amplitude-level summation preserves branch coherence; the light-front quasienergy phase Delta_phi_n(p) accumulated over half a cycle creates time-domain double-slit interference - extrema at (p^2/2 + Ip + A_n^2/2)/Lambda = N*omega, constructive only for N = n mod 2 due to the (-1)^n factor - neighboring photon-number channels enhance complementary ATI combs
- Longitudinal momentum thus encodes BOTH the photon statistics (via the scaling anomaly) and the field coherence (via parity modulation) - a two-channel quantum-light diagnostic in a single observable
- Context: builds on the recent experimental demonstration of BSV-driven tunneling ionization; residual deviations from a near-universal sigma_pz-<p_z> relation attributed to subleading contributions three and five orders of magnitude smaller

### 潜在研究方向（Future Directions）

Experimental test of the I^(2/3) law at BSV facilities; extension of the nondipole quantum diagnostic to solids (longitudinal momentum in solid HHG photoemission); connection to quantum-light HHG programs
