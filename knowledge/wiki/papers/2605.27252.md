# Real-Time Time-Dependent Density Functional Theory for Pump-Probe Spectroscopies

**arXiv**: [2605.27252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27252) | **方向**: 阿秒科学 / 强场

---

## 研究问题（Research Question）

This is a review chapter that consolidates, in one coherent formalism, the simulation and interpretation of attosecond pump-probe spectroscopies within real-time TDDFT, covering non-relativistic (1c), exact-two-component amfX2C (2c), and fully four-component Dirac-Coulomb (4c) Hamiltonians on equal footing. The contribution is twofold. First, it provides a unified real-time protocol for two distinct ultrafast spectroscopies, transient absorption (TAS) and transient electronic circular dichroism (TCD), using differential induced dipole/magnetic-dipole moments and δ-type broadband probe pulses. Second, it derives a generalized non-equilibrium response function whose imaginary part gives the TAS/TCD cross-section and which transparently attributes transient spectral signatures (delay-dependent oscillations, negative peaks, quantum beating) to quantum coherences in the pump-prepared wavepacket. Crucially, the theory is extended to complex-valued spinor bases, yielding an additional contribution proportional to Im(Pv,km Pu,mj) that vanishes identically in the real-orbital non-relativistic limit but is essential for spin-orbit-split core-level spectroscopy and light-induced chirality in heavy-element systems. By demonstrating that the cheap amfX2C Hamiltonian reproduces gold-standard 4c Dirac-Coulomb results, the chapter establishes amfX2C as a practical workhorse for routine attosecond simulations of valence and core spectroscopies. It also proposes light-induced persistent electronic chirality in achiral molecules as a designable phenomenon, opening a new axis for ultrafast chiral control.

## 研究背景（Background）

This chapter sits squarely at the intersection of two of Lin Sai's core interests: attosecond spectroscopy of electronic coherence in solids/molecules and the symmetry/spin-orbit structure of nonlinear light-matter interaction. The non-equilibrium response formalism provides an explicit mathematical bridge from electron density and induced dipole moments to experimentally measurable attosecond spectra, which is precisely the theoretical machinery needed to interpret the kind of solid-state HHG and attosecond pump-probe data Lin Sai works with (ZnO, TMD heterostructures, altermagnets). Several concrete transfer vectors are immediately relevant: (i) The relativistic Im(Pv,km Pu,mj) correction and amfX2C Hamiltonian offer a practical route to incorporate spin-orbit coupling into real-time TDDFT simulations of HHG-active materials containing heavy elements (W, Mo, Te, Bi), which are central to spin-current HHG in altermagnets; this complements Lin Sai's existing interest in HHG spin currents (cf. Mizuno 2606.31573) by giving a first-principles tool to predict, rather than just symmetry-classify, the relativistic HHG response. (ii) The light-induced persistent electronic chirality mechanism (single CP pulse on achiral furan yields long-lived mirror-image chiral wavepackets) is a molecular analogue of the optical-control-of-magnetism problem Lin Sai pursues in solids, and it offers a controllable testbed for studying how spin-orbit coupling and relativistic phases (the Im(Pv,km Pu,mj) term) generate and stabilize chiral electronic order on attosecond timescales. (iii) The transient electronic circular dichroism observable, derived from induced magnetic dipole moments, could be ported to crystalline systems to probe light-induced valley polarization and chirality in TMDs and altermagnets with relativistic Hamiltonians, where current work uses charge/spin currents—TCD would provide an independent, complementary channel. (iv) The 800 nm Ti:Sapphire compatibility issue is mitigated because the theory is wavelength-agnostic; only the carrier frequency in the pulse enters the formalism, so all the analysis transfers to mid-IR (Lin Sai's preferred HHG driving regime) without modification. (v) The MO-basis representation of induced charge and current densities via ρ_ind(r,t) = Tr[D_ind Ω(r)] and j_ind,k(r,t) = Tr[D_ind J_k(r)] provides a natural real-space observable for visualizing attosecond electron flow in solids, complementing Lin Sai's interest in real-time microscopic pictures of ultrafast processes. A natural next step would be for Lin Sai to combine this RT-TDDFT + amfX2C framework with the spin-current HHG selection-rule formalism of Mizuno (2606.31573) to simulate, with explicit SO coupling, the spin-current HHG fingerprint of altermagnets such as RuO2 or MnTe, using the induced current density j_ind as the bridge between the TDDFT dynamics and the spin-current observable.

## 研究方法（Methods）

- Unified RT-TDDFT protocol for attosecond transient absorption and transient electronic circular dichroism at three Hamiltonian levels (1c, 2c amfX2C, 4c Dirac-Coulomb) with explicit finite pump pulses (sin·cos² linearly polarized or Gaussian-enveloped circularly polarized) and δ-type broadband probe, using differential induced dipole/magnetic-dipole observables to isolate the pump-induced non-stationary wavepacket response.
- Analytical derivation of a generalized non-equilibrium response function Im χ_PuPv[E](ω, T+τ) for transient absorption and the analogous Im χ_MuPv[E](ω, T+τ) for transient ECD, with explicit decomposition into Lorentzian absorptive L(ω) and Rayleigh dispersive R(ω) lineshapes weighted by time-delay-dependent phase factors sin/cos(Θ_jk(τ)), and an explicit relativistic correction term proportional to Im(Pv,km Pu,mj) that is non-zero only for intrinsically complex spinor wave functions (4c Dirac or 2c amfX2C). This is the first systematic connection between attosecond TAS/TCD spectral features (negative peaks, beating, delay-dependent oscillations) and the underlying quantum coherences of the pump-prepared wavepacket within a relativistic real-time framework.
- Dipole-weighted transition analysis (DWTA) as a post-processing tool to attribute TAS spectral peaks to specific occupied-virtual spinor-pair transitions by Fourier-transforming time-domain induced dipoles after applying a Lorentzian damping factor exp(-Γt), enabling unambiguous orbital decomposition of transient spectral features.
- Use of atomic mean-field exact two-component (amfX2C) Hamiltonian with picture-change correction ΔF_amfX2C_⊕ built from a superposition of atomic SCF contributions, as a computationally tractable surrogate for the full 4c Dirac-Coulomb Hamiltonian. Demonstrated to match 4c results for both core-level (thiophene S L2,3-edge) and valence-level (tellurophene TCD) attosecond spectroscopies while reducing 4c two-electron integral cost (which is ~25× the 1c cost) to affordable levels.
- Pseudo-observable framework: induced charge density ρ_ind(r,t) = Tr[D_ind Ω(r)] and induced current density j_ind,k(r,t) = Tr[D_ind J_k(r)] (k∈{x,y,z}) computed from the induced density matrix D_ind = D(t) - D0 in the ground-state MO basis, providing direct real-space visualization of pump-driven electron flow during attosecond processes. The 4c relativistic generalization uses ψ†Σ_u ψ for charge/spin density and -c ψ†α_k ψ for current density with Dirac alpha matrices.

## 主要发现与核心结论（Main Results）

- The time-dependent Kohn-Sham system reduces to a Liouville-von Neumann equation iℏ ∂D/∂t = [F(t), D(t)] for the one-electron reduced density matrix D(t) = C(t)C†(t) in an orthonormal atomic-orbital basis, and D is invariant under unitary rotations within the occupied subspace, so that the entire single-determinant electron dynamics is encoded in D(t). This makes density-matrix propagation the natural real-time formulation for pump-probe simulations, since the Fock matrix depends self-consistently on D at the same time step.
- The unified pump-probe protocol computes differential induced dipoles Δμ_TAS(t) = μ_ind,pp - μ_ind,p (and the magnetic analogue for TCD), Fourier-transforms them after exponential damping Γ, and converts to TAS cross-section S_TAS(ω) = (4πω/3c) Im Tr[α_TAS] with α = Δμ(ω)/F0, or to molar extinction coefficient Δε_TCD(ω) = (20 N_A ω / (3 ln10) c² ε0) Im Tr[β_TCD] with β = -i Δm(ω)/F0. The δ-type probe (Eq. 19) excites all frequencies simultaneously, providing broadband response from a single propagation.
- For transient absorption, the generalized nonequilibrium response function Im χ_PuPv[E](ω, T+τ) contains four hallmark terms absent in equilibrium linear response: (i) τ-dependent phase modulation cos(Θ_jk(τ)) and sin(Θ_jk(τ)) with Θ_jk(τ) = φ_j - φ_k - ω_jk τ produces spectral oscillations with pump-probe delay; (ii) interference between positive- and negative-frequency components yields negative-intensity peaks; (iii) diagonal (j=k) terms give a τ-independent background; (iv) off-diagonal (j≠k) terms capture quantum beating of the wavepacket. When the initial state reduces to a single eigenstate (c_j = δ_j0), the nonequilibrium structure collapses to conventional equilibrium response.
- A previously hidden relativistic contribution, proportional to Im(Pv,km Pu,mj), appears in TAS and TCD response functions when intrinsically complex spinor wave functions are used (Dirac 4c or amfX2C 2c). This term vanishes identically for real-orbital non-relativistic calculations and is responsible for spin-orbit-allowed pathways that cannot be captured by 1c Hamiltonians. It is essential for the S 2p spin-orbit splitting observed in thiophene L2,3-edge X-ray TAS and for the relativistic reshaping of TCD in tellurophene.
- Ethylene TAS (ω0 = 0.2762 au, E0 = 0.01 au, δ-probe F0 = 0.01 au) exhibits negative-intensity peaks and τ-oscillatory spectral intensity. Dipole-weighted transition analysis (DWTA) identifies that peaks A, B, C arise predominantly from two degenerate spinor-pair excitations (15,16)→(19,20) and (13,14)→(46,45). A simplified two-level version of Eq. 36 reproduces the essential beating pattern from coherent interference alone, showing that the τ-oscillation arises from wavepacket coherence rather than orbital dynamics.
- Thiophene S L2,3-edge X-ray TAS requires relativistic Hamiltonians: 1c non-relativistic misses the 2p spin-orbit splitting entirely, while 2c amfX2C and 4c Dirac-Coulomb produce indistinguishable spectra with clear SO doublets. This validates amfX2C as a faithful and computationally cheaper surrogate for 4c Dirac-Coulomb in attosecond core spectroscopy.
- Circularly polarized pump light on oriented achiral furan induces a persistent, mirror-image chiral wavepacket detectable by transient electronic circular dichroism: left- and right-CP pumps yield TCD spectra that are strict mirror images, and the induced charge ρ_ind(r,T) and current j_ind(r,T) densities at the end of the pump are non-superimposable mirror images. Different spectral regions exhibit different sign-flip timescales, linked to multiple harmonic orders of the carrier frequency in the induced magnetic dipole Fourier transform—no single global chirality-flip time exists.
- In tellurophene, replacing furan with the heavier Te analogue requires relativistic treatment for quantitative TCD even in the valence region: SO coupling dramatically reshapes the spectroscopic response, and again 2c amfX2C matches 4c Dirac-Coulomb quantitatively. This extends the amfX2C validation from core-level (thiophene S-edge) to valence-level chirality in heavy-element systems.
- Four acknowledged limitations of the framework as presented: (1) localized AO basis cannot describe ionization into the continuum—requires complex absorbing potentials or continuum basis; (2) closed-system LvN conserves purity and lacks decoherence from vibrations, solvent, bath; (3) frozen-nuclei Born-Oppenheimer approximation is only valid in the first few femtoseconds, beyond which Ehrenfest/surface-hopping/MCTDH is needed; (4) adiabatic approximation for v_xc neglects memory effects, inheriting all well-known linear-response TDDFT failure modes (e.g., charge-transfer excitations).

## 潜在研究方向（Future Directions）

- Extend the framework beyond frozen nuclei by coupling RT-TDDFT electron propagation to nuclear dynamics through mean-field Ehrenfest, surface hopping, or MCTDH approaches, enabling simulations at delays longer than a few femtoseconds where nuclear motion begins to modulate the electronic wavepacket. The nuclear-electronic orbital (NEO) Ehrenfest method and the Li-Tully-Schlegel-Frisch ab initio Ehrenfest are natural starting points (refs 57, 100, 101).
- Embed the closed-system LvN propagation within an open quantum system framework that couples the electronic subsystem to a bath, capturing decoherence from vibrational motions, solvent, and radiative decay. Established routes exist via time-dependent DFT for open quantum systems (refs 96-99) and solid-state decoherence-corrected TDDFT (ref 98).
- Go beyond the adiabatic exchange-correlation approximation by incorporating memory effects (non-adiabatic v_xc), addressing the well-known failure modes of TDDFT for charge-transfer excitations (ref 104) and double excitations. This is critical for extending TAS/TCD fidelity to systems where conventional ALDA/PBE functionals are known to be qualitatively wrong.
- Address the LOCAO basis limitation for continuum processes by combining the presented TAS/TCD framework with complex absorbing potentials or specialized continuum basis functions (refs 89-95), enabling simulation of strong-field ionization, high-harmonic generation, and above-threshold ionization within the same relativistic real-time infrastructure.
- Design experimental pump-probe schemes exploiting light-induced persistent electronic chirality in achiral molecules: the prediction that a single monochromatic CP pulse induces a long-lived chiral wavepacket detectable by attosecond TAS opens pathways for ultrafast chiral state control in spintronics and enantioselective chemistry. Concrete next steps: identify molecular systems where this signal survives decoherence, explore multi-pulse schemes for active chirality manipulation, and propose detection schemes beyond TCD.
- Systematically survey heavy-element chiral molecules (chalcogenophene series furan → thiophene → selenophene → tellurophene) to map the SO-coupling-driven TCD reshaping as a function of atomic number, using amfX2C as a practical Hamiltonian to generate a transferable database of relativistic chirality signatures.
- Apply the same generalized nonequilibrium response framework to other pump-probe observables (e.g., transient X-ray diffraction, transient photoelectron, transient absorption with circularly polarized probe) and to multi-pulse sequences beyond the simple pump-δ-probe protocol currently analyzed, including pump-pump-probe and chirped-pulse geometries.
