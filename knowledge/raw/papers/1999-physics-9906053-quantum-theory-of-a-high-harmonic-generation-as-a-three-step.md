# Quantum theory of a high harmonic generation as a three-step process

## 摘要（原文 Abstract）

Fully quantum treatment explicitly presents the high harmonic generation as a three-step process: (i) above threshold ionization (ATI) is followed by (ii) electron propagation in a laser-dressed continuum. Subsequently (iii) stimulated (or laser assisted) recombination brings the electron back into the initial state with emission of a high-energy photon. Contributions of all ATI channels add up coherently. All three stages of the process are described by simple, mostly analytical expressions that allow a detailed physical interpretation. A very good quantitative agreement with the previous calculations on the harmonic generation by H-minus ion is demonstrated, thus supplementing the conceptual significance of the theory with its practical efficiency. The virtue of the present scheme is further supported by a good accord between the calculations in length and velocity gauges for the high-energy photon.

## 关键科学问题

- The novel contribution is establishing a quantitative, quantum-mechanically consistent relationship between above-threshold ionization (ATI) and high harmonic generation (HG) by incorporating electron-core interactions beyond the standard Corkum model, thereby resolving the long-standing lack of a unified theoretical framework linking these two phenomena.

## 核心发现与创新点

- This section derives the dipole matrix element for harmonic generation by expressing the field-dressed initial and final atomic states using the retarded Green function and solving the time-dependent Schrödinger equation in the presence of a laser field.
- This section introduces a factorization technique based on Kuchiev's method to simplify the analysis of high Fourier components in high-harmonic generation by transforming the time-dependent Green function integral into a summation over ATI channels, enabling closed-form integration and avoiding the pole approximation.
- The adiabatic approximation simplifies the calculation of above-threshold ionization amplitudes by evaluating the integral via the saddle-point method, where saddle points correspond to singularities in the bound-state wave function and are determined by energy conservation and the condition that the phase derivative vanishes, with the approach relying on the asymptotic behavior of the wave function for self-consistency.
- The theory reproduces the high-harmonic generation rates and plateau-region structures (including dips and peaks) of H⁻ in a laser field with excellent agreement for large $N$, particularly in the fall-off region, where it matches Becker et al.'s results, while showing good average agreement in the plateau region despite minor discrepancies in peak magnitudes and deviations at smaller $N$.

## 总结与展望

This paper presents a quantum mechanical, three-step model of high-harmonic generation—absorption, rescattering, and recombination—where each step corresponds to a physically realizable process, offering a simple, exact, and practical framework for calculating harmonic yields.
