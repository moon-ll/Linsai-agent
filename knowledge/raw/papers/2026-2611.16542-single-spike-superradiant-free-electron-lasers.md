## 摘要

Free-electron lasers (FELs) deliver high-brilliance radiation from the infrared to X-rays, enabling atomic-scale studies on femtosecond-to-attosecond timescales. Yet conventional facilities rely on large, costly radio-frequency accelerators. Laser wakefield accelerators (LWFAs), combining high accelerating gradients with ultra-high peak currents, offer a compact alternative and open up new prospects for advanced FEL operation. Here, we demonstrate a tabletop superradiant FEL driven by single-spike, pre-bunched electron beams from a LWFA. By manipulating the energy chirp during acceleration and compressing the bunch to sub-wavelength duration, we obtain coherent radiation extending into the vacuum-ultraviolet (VUV) regime within a remarkably compact 3-metre beamline--from gas target to radiator. Under optimized conditions, the measured photon yield reaches 2.8x10^10 per pulse after transmission through a 500-um slit, with a reproducibility of approximately 70%. This approach uniquely exploits the energy chirp and high-current characteristics inherent to LWFA beams without recourse to exponential gain, substantially minimizing device length while obviating the need for external synchronization and offering enhanced tolerance to jitter--thereby establishing a practical route toward efficient, compact FELs suitable for applications in academic, industrial, and clinical settings.
