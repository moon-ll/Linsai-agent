# Giant enhancement of attosecond tunnel ionization competes with disorder-driven decoherence in amorphized silicon

## 摘要

High-harmonic generation (HHG) and tunnel ionization in solids are strong-field phenomena sensitive to the attosecond dynamics of laser-driven electron-hole pairs. While HHG has been used extensively as a spectroscopic probe in crystalline systems, its potential as a probe of disorder on the length scale of electron-hole trajectories remains relatively unexplored. Furthermore, with the advent of petahertz electronics, there is a growing need for controlling ionization on the nanoscale. Here, we show how focused-ion beam driven amorphization of silicon leads to a giant enhancement ($>$250 times) of yield for lower-order harmonics and quenching of higher-order harmonics, consistent with a dramatically enhanced tunnel ionization yield and disorder-induced decoherence, respectively. Modelling the real-space quantum dynamics, we show how electron-hole coherence in the amorphous phase is lost over a characteristic length of approximately five lattice sites. HHG spectroscopy also reveals remnant order inherited from the original cubic structure. Our results establish HHG spectroscopy as a probe of medium-range order and pave the way for new opportunities in lightwave nanoelectronics.
