# Altermagnetism-Induced Spin-resolved electronic structure in Janus FeX0.5Y0.5 Monolayers (X, Y = S, Se, Te)

## Abstract (原文摘要)
Realizing the spin-resolved electronic properties in superconducting materials stands as a critical frontier, offering both novel fundamental physics and potential for dissipationless spin-based devices. Here, we predict a series of Janus FeX0.5Y0.5 monolayers derived from iron-based superconductors (e.g., FeSe, FeTe, and FeS) by using Kondo-type model and first-principles calculations. These Janus structures exhibit significant spin-splittingelectronic states, large topological band gaps (51.4 meV) and high Néel temperatures (415 K). We further reveal that valley polarization can be effectively tuned via applied in-plane strain and the resulting valley-polarized anomalous Hall conductivity can be manipulated by shifting the Fermi level. Our work suggests a new strategy based on altermagnetism for engineering spin-splitting states in superconducting systems and inspires further exploration of superconducting spintronics.

## Background and Motivation (背景概述与问题引入)
In the condensed matter frontier, combining spin-resolved electronic structures with superconductivity is a critical challenge. Conventional ferromagnetic-superconducting hybrids suffer from suppressed superconductivity due to net magnetization. The recently proposed concept of altermagnetism offers a new route: spin-split bands without net magnetization. This work targets Janus FeX0.5Y0.5 monolayers derived from iron-based superconductors (FeSe, FeTe, FeS), engineering broken out-of-plane mirror symmetry to induce strong spin splitting while keeping the parent compound non-magnetic.

## Key Findings (关键发现及核心讨论)
- **Finding 1**: Kondo-type model + first-principles calculations predict sizable spin splitting in Janus FeX0.5Y0.5 monolayers, distinct from ferromagnets yet functionally spin-polarized.
- **Finding 2**: Topological gap reaches **51.4 meV**, Neel temperature up to **415 K** -- promising for room-temperature spintronic applications.
- **Finding 3**: In-plane strain effectively modulates valley polarization magnitude, enabling on-demand valley engineering.
- **Finding 4**: The valley-polarized anomalous Hall conductivity can be tuned by shifting the Fermi level, providing a new degree of freedom for device design.

## Main Methods (主要方法)
- **Kondo-type tight-binding model**: Captures d-orbital strong correlations and spin structure at the model level.
- **First-principles DFT calculations**: Predicts band structure, magnetic ground state, topological character, and strain response.
- **Symmetry analysis**: Broken out-of-plane mirror symmetry explained as the physical origin of spin splitting.

## Innovation (创新性)
First introduction of altermagnetism into iron-based superconductor-derived systems via Janus asymmetric substitution, achieving spin-split states without external magnetic fields -- distinct from conventional magnetic doping or field-tunable schemes.

## Conclusions and Outlook (结论与展望)
Proposes a new altermagnetism-based strategy to engineer spin-split states in superconducting systems, inspiring dissipationless spin-based devices and superconducting spintronics. Follow-up work likely targets experimental synthesis (MBE / CVD) and verification of superconductivity-altermagnetism coexistence.
