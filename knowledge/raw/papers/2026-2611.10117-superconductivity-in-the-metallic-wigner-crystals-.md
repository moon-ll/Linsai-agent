## 摘要

Recent experiments on rhombohedral multilayer graphene have reported metallic Wigner crystals (MWCs), in which spontaneously broken translational symmetry coexists with mobile charge carriers. Their proximity to time-reversal-symmetry-breaking superconductivity raises the intriguing possibility that collective excitations of the electron crystal mediate pairing among the residual itinerant carriers, giving rise to a superconducting Wigner crystal---an electronic analog of a supersolid. Here we investigate this possibility using a realistic model of electron-doped rhombohedral multilayer graphene with spin and valley polarization, whose low symmetry removes the usual Cooper instability. We use self-consistent and time-dependent Hartree-Fock theory to map the evolution of the self-doping fraction and characterize the collective-mode spectrum of the MWCs, followed by a multiband linearized Eliashberg analysis of pairing. Upon increasing density, we find an instability of the MWC, triggered by a weakly dispersing unoccupied band approaching the chemical potential. Pairing remains weak deep in the stable MWC. However, on approaching the instability, we identify a collective mode composed of interband electron-hole excitations that softens. We show that this mode drives pairing at experimentally relevant temperatures. The leading pairing instability is at finite momentum and combines Fermi surface states with states in the incipient band, making a Fermi surface or single-band pairing analysis inadequate. Our results establish collective-mode-mediated superconductivity in MWCs as a viable scenario for rhombohedral graphene and motivate experimental searches for coexisting electronic charge order in its superconducting phases.
