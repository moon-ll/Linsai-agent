## 摘要

Circularly polarized light, with its specific handedness, is a valuable tool for investigating the chirality related properties of materials, including their structural, electronic, magnetic and topological characteristics. This has led to efforts in creating wavelength-tunable coherent light with circular or elliptical polarization through a sub-cycle ultrafast process high-harmonic generation (HHG). Existing approaches to chiral HHG, however, typically rely on multidimensional driving fields, materials engineering or complex optical architectures. Here we uncover a distinct mechanism without such complexity, where a single linearly polarized driving field generates chiral HHG from a pristine crystal. We show that, in the Weyl semimetal NbP, one linearly polarized near-infrared driving field generates high harmonics with pronounced ellipticity. Semi-conductor Bloch equations (SBEs) reveal that this ellipticity originates from the coherent superposition of multiple interband transitions, each contributing harmonics with distinct linear polarizations and relative phases. We further substantiate this mechanism by shifting the driving photon energy into the mid-infrared, which strongly suppresses the ellipticity as the number of accessible interband transition channels is reduced. These results establish a simple and general mechanism for chiral HHG, and show that quantum materials can transform an apparently achiral driving field into polarization-tunable ultrafast chiral emission.
