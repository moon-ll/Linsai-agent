# Self-probing of Molecules with High Harmonic Generation

## 摘要（原文 Abstract）

This tutorial presents the most important aspects of the molecular self-probing paradigm, which views the process of high harmonic generation as "a molecule being probed by one of its own electrons". Since the properties of the electron wavepacket acting as a probe allow a combination of attosecond and \ ngstr\\"om resolutions in measurements, this idea bears great potential for the observation, and possibly control, of ultrafast quantum dynamics in molecules at the electronic level. Theoretical as well as experimental methods and concepts at the basis of self-probing measurements are introduced. Many of these are discussed on the example of molecular orbital tomography.

## 主要方法

- The key finding is that consistent model predictions across redundant experimental data enable reliable extraction of physically relevant parameters, but this approach requires either highly accurate ab initio models or strong prior knowledge to avoid overfitting and ensure meaningful parameter inference.
- The experiment demonstrates that near-perfect molecular alignment and excellent orientation can be achieved in low-density, rotationally cold gas jets, enabling high-fidelity control of molecular orientation for HHG, though a trade-off between low rotational temperature and sufficient medium density limits performance in high-density HHG media.
- The experimental study demonstrates that the complex XUV spectrum of N₂ under HHG is accurately described by a coherent sum of contributions from the HOMO and HOMO-1 orbitals, with a measured recombination DME showing a total intensity consistent with theoretical predictions, where the HOMO contribution dominates due to its higher ionization probability and similar ionization potential to argon, while the HOMO-1 contribution is significantly smaller but still detectable.
