## 摘要

In bilayer WSe2 the valence-band maxima at K and Γ lie within a few thermal energies of one another, and gating redistributes holes between a light and a heavy valley of different mobilities on a landscape that stays fixed. We show that rhombohedral (R) stacking moves the landscape itself. The three-fold symmetry of R stacking forbids interlayer tunneling at K but not at Γ. The K coupling is therefore exactly zero, not merely small. A fit that leaves the coupling free returns an upper bound of 0.017 meV, three orders of magnitude under k_BT at room temperature, while the Γ coupling is 361.8 meV. The polarization creates a potential step between the layers and splits the K doublet. Each K state stays on a single layer, so the K edge shifts by half of that splitting, 41.7 meV, which any tunneling would reduce. Γ barely responds. Two consequences follow from symmetry alone, whatever the size of that shift. The two registries, carrying opposite out-of-plane ferroelectric polarizations, are exact mirror images along the displacement-field axis, and reversing the polarization reverses the displacement of the landscape. Their differential-mode transconductances are equal and opposite at zero field, where the response vanishes for an ideal nonpolar reference. The stored polarization is read out as a reversal of sign and not as a difference of current. Sweeping the field is a second route to the same parity. In the ideal symmetric limit the transconductance passes through zero at the field that cancels the polarization, 1.07 V/nm, at a single temperature. Both routes are limited by the symmetry of the device more than by the band structure. Stacking also renormalizes the landscape, so the polar one is not a translated copy of the nonpolar reference. Absolute offsets follow the exchange-correlation functional. The symmetry relations do not.
