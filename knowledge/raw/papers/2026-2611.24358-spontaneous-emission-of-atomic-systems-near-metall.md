## 摘要

We investigate the spontaneous emission of a quantum emitter placed near finite atomic chains described by the Su-Schrieffer-Heeger model. By tuning the chain from a metallic to a semiconducting character through dimerization, we analyze how the electronic structure of an atomic-scale scatterer, together with the illumination profile, determines the strength, bandwidth, and spatial structure of the spontaneous emission enhancement. The resonant enhancement depends on both spectral matching and the emitter position relative to the induced charge and field patterns. In metallic chains, the nearly harmonic energy spectrum leads to overlapping optical transitions and strong resonances. For semiconducting chains, dimerization opens a gap and leads to a modified density of electronic states, redistributing the response over a broader spectral range and reducing its peak intensity. Coulomb interactions affect the optical resonances through blueshift or modification of their spatial character, and act differently on collective and single-particle-like excitations. Finally, we show that the same mechanisms demonstrated in atomic chains persist in realistic quasi-one-dimensional systems, including trans-polyacetylene chains and graphene nanoribbons. Our findings indicate a general direction for engineering light-matter interactions at the atomic scale, and may offer design principles for tunable nanoscale photonic and quantum-optical devices.
