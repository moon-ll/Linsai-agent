# High-harmonic generation along a structural phase transition in silicon

## 摘要

We explore high-harmonic generation (HHG) in silicon treated by a variable dose from a Ga focused ion beam (Ga$^+$ FIB). We identify four qualitatively different dose regimes as the sample transitions from a crystalline to amorphous phase. The phase transition manifests as a giant enhancement in yield of low-order harmonics, which is strongly correlated with surface swelling as measured by atomic force microscopy. Below the critical dose, we observe a mixed phase regime where the HHG spectra display elements of both crystalline and amorphous silicon. We show how the samples can be laser-annealed with intense non-resonant pulses, where energy is deposited preferentially into amorphous regions, allowing us to disentangle the relative importance of chemical and structural modifications to the strong-field dynamics. At very high dose, we observe an increased sensitivity to laser-induced damage, which is correlated with FIB-induced etching. Energy dispersive x-ray spectroscopy reveals that laser damage leads to the formation of Ga-rich nanoparticles and porosity at the surface.
